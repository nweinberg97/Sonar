Forms get the polite version.

Typing is work, so people keep it short. Talking is easy, so they tell you what happened, what got in the way and what they wish was different. Sonar is built to test one idea: saying it out loud is easier than filling in a survey, and you learn more from it.

How it works

  1. 1

    Ask one question

    Write the question and what you want to learn. Sonar uses your goal to choose follow-ups. Respondents never see it.

  2. 2

    They talk, Sonar listens

    One tap and they talk. When they pause, a short follow-up appears so they keep going. Any phone or laptop, no app, no account.

  3. 3

    You get the signal

    Every answer becomes text, a sentiment and a theme. Insights pulls them together: what you heard, what keeps coming up, and what to do next.

Read one page, know what to do next.

You don’t have to listen to anything. Sonar writes the summary, counts the themes across everyone, and turns them into a short to-do list. Every theme comes with what people actually said.

Built for teams that keep listening.

A one-off survey tells you how things were. Sonar is meant to run all the time, in the places people already are, and get better at asking the longer it runs. Here’s what exists today and what’s coming.

  • Works today

    A weekly pulse

    Same link every week. See what's coming up more, less, or for the first time, per team.

  • Works today

    One name per idea

    A shared theme library across every Sonar, so counts add up over time.

  • Works today

    Slack and Linear

    Send a summary to a channel or turn a request into an issue. You review it before it goes.

  • Works today

    Testimonials, with permission

    People can say “you can quote me.” You choose which quotes to use.

  • Works today

    Inside your product

    A Feedback button any site can add with one line, offered right after a moment that matters. Try the one in the corner.

  • Later

    Closing the loop

    Personal replies to people who ask for one, and your CRM remembering what they told you.

Built to be trusted

Voice is never stored
Audio becomes text on the server and is thrown away. Only the words are kept.
Anonymous by default
No names, emails or accounts for the people answering.
Your own server, open-source models
Transcription and analysis run on open-source models where Sonar runs, not a third-party AI service.
A person approves what leaves
Nothing goes to Slack, Linear or a website until someone reads it and presses send.
